## How We Picked

Consumer Reports’ labs evaluate luggage for the things travelers actually feel: **usability** (handles, zippers, interior layout), **impact resistance**, **carrying and pulling comfort**, **construction quality**, **ergonomics and design**, and **safety**. In addition, CR’s in-depth member surveys (20,000+ respondents) show which brands hold up over years of trips—highlighting that **durability** and **wheelability** matter most to satisfaction. We combined those test and survey signals with hands-on impressions, warranty policies, and current street pricing to finalize the list below.

## Carry-On Buying Tips (What Matters Most)

*   **Wheelability first.** In CR’s surveys, wheelability is the #1 factor tied to owner satisfaction. Look for large, smooth-rolling spinners and a handle that extends high enough to avoid heel-strike.
*   **Durability is worth paying for.** Zippers, corner guards, and handle tubes are the failure points. Premium brands (Briggs & Riley, Rimowa, Travelpro’s higher lines) use parts you can repair instead of replace.
*   **Hard vs. soft shell.** Hard-shells resist crushing and keep a cleaner silhouette; soft-sides flex into tight bins and often give you useful exterior pockets. If you gate-check often, a tougher hard-shell helps.
*   **Mind the size rules.** U.S. domestic limits hover around 22″ x 14″ x 9″ (including wheels/handles). International carriers are stricter; if you fly overseas, consider a “global” 21″.
*   **Warranty = real value.** A lifetime policy that covers airline damage can save hundreds over years of travel. Briggs & Riley leads here; Travelpro and Samsonite policies vary by line.

## FAQ

### Which carry-on brand lasts the longest?
Consumer Reports’ member surveys consistently reward **Briggs & Riley** and **Travelpro** for longevity and support. Among hard-shells, **Samsonite** and premium brands like **Rimowa** perform well for impact resistance.

### Is a hard-shell or soft-side better for carry-on?
Hard-shells protect delicate items and look newer longer; soft-sides are easier to stuff and access during boarding. If you travel with camera gear or often gate-check, pick hard-shell. If you overpack sweaters and want exterior pockets, pick soft-side.

### What features are worth it?
Double spinner wheels, a sturdy telescoping handle, lockable zippers, and an interior compression panel. USB ports are handy but rarely essential; prioritize build quality over gadgets.
