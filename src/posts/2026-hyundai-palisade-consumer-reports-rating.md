# Here's Why Consumer Reports Rated the 2026 Hyundai Palisade So Highly

![2026 Hyundai Palisade Hybrid Front](/images/cars/hyundai-palisade-2026-front.jpg)

If you are shopping for a three-row family SUV, the Hyundai Palisade has probably popped up in your search results. For its second generation in 2026, Hyundai went big. They increased the size, upgraded the interior, and—most importantly—introduced a brand-new Hybrid powertrain. 

But does it actually live up to the hype? 

According to the strict, independent testing at Consumer Reports, the answer is a resounding yes. The **2026 Hyundai Palisade Hybrid** earned a massive Overall Score of 88 out of 100, easily securing a "CR Recommended" badge. Even more impressive? It earned a near-perfect 95/100 on its official Road Test. 

Here at CR Watchdog, we cut through the dealership marketing to look at the hard data. Here is exactly why Consumer Reports rated the new Palisade Hybrid so highly, and the few hidden flaws you need to know about before you buy. 

## The Highs: Why It Dominated the Lab Tests

When you are paying between $44,160 and $58,780 for a family vehicle, you expect it to perform. The Palisade Hybrid delivered in almost every major testing category.

### 1. Luxury-Level Quiet and Comfort

You do not have to buy a Mercedes or a BMW to get a premium cabin. Consumer Reports noted that the Palisade's interior is significantly more hushed than most three-row SUVs, keeping road and wind noise to an absolute minimum. If you step up to the top-tier Calligraphy trim, you are treated to Nappa leather, a suede-like headliner, and even a massage function in the front seats.

### 2. Serious Passing Power
Normally, buying a hybrid means sacrificing speed for fuel efficiency. Not here. The Palisade Hybrid pairs a 2.5-liter turbocharged four-cylinder engine with two electric motors to generate a massive 329 horsepower. It sprints from 0 to 60 mph in a quick 7.0 seconds flat—leaving the gas-only V6 Palisade (which takes 8.4 seconds) in the dust. 

### 3. Straightforward Controls

In an era where automakers are burying every simple function inside complicated touchscreens, the Palisade remains refreshingly logical. It features a large, clutter-free infotainment screen, but it keeps physical knobs for audio volume, tuning, and temperature controls. You don't have to take your eyes off the road just to turn down the AC. 

### 4. A Suite of Standard Safety
Safety shouldn't be a luxury add-on. The Palisade comes standard with critical active safety features, including Automatic Emergency Braking (AEB) with pedestrian detection, Blind Spot Warning (BSW), and Rear Cross Traffic Warning (RCTW). It even features a "Safe Exit Assist" system that stops you from opening your door into traffic if a bicycle or car is coming up beside you. 

## The Watchdog Reality Check: The Lows

A score of 88 is fantastic, but no car is perfect. The Consumer Reports test drivers noted a few distinct flaws that you need to be aware of before taking one for a test drive.

### 1. Disappointing Hybrid Fuel Economy
This is the biggest letdown. You buy a hybrid to save money at the gas pump. However, the all-wheel-drive Palisade Hybrid only managed 26 MPG overall in CR's real-world testing. That is only a 6-MPG improvement over the standard gas-guzzling version. For context, the rival Toyota Grand Highlander Hybrid completely trounces the Palisade by getting 35 MPG overall. 

### 2. Clunky Low-Speed Shifting
While the Palisade is incredibly fast when you floor it, it struggles a bit in stop-and-go traffic. Testers noted that when you try to accelerate from a rolling stop, the powertrain suffers from a slight hesitation. The handoff between the electric motors and the gas engine can feel abrupt and clunky at low speeds, making the traditional gas-only V6 Palisade feel a bit smoother around town. 

### 3. The Third-Row Squeeze
While the second-row captain's chairs are incredibly comfortable, the third row is a different story. Accessing the third row is a tight squeeze because the power-operated second-row seats move very slowly. Once you are back there, the bottom seat cushion is very low to the floor, forcing adults into an uncomfortable knee-bend. 

### 4. Average Predicted Reliability
Hyundai still struggles to match the bulletproof reliability of brands like Toyota and Honda. Based on owner surveys, the 2026 Palisade Hybrid holds a Predicted Reliability score of 48/100. That is solidly middle-of-the-pack—not terrible, but not a guarantee of a perfectly repair-free decade, either. 

## The Bottom Line

The **2026 Hyundai Palisade Hybrid** is a phenomenal piece of engineering that earned its 88 Overall Score. It rides beautifully, features a gorgeous interior, and packs serious acceleration power. 

However, you have to know what you are buying. If your absolute top priority is getting the highest MPG possible, you should go look at the Toyota Grand Highlander Hybrid instead. But if you want a premium, near-luxury driving experience with an intuitive dashboard and plenty of passing power, the Palisade Hybrid is one of the best SUVs on the market today.
